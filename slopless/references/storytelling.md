# Storytelling: make evidence carry the idea

Load for new stories, redesigns that change composition, or a critique of comprehension. Existing source copy is fixed unless rewriting is authorized.

## Argument before appearance

Write one sentence: who needs to understand or do what, because of which evidence? Keep this working sentence outside the deliverable unless it is useful audience copy. A product task uses the same logic: context → decision → action → feedback.

For each view identify its claim, evidence and takeaway. Evidence can be a source number, screenshot, sequence, quote or observable product behavior. A headline makes a claim the evidence actually supports. If the source is incomplete, expose that gap; never invent proof or a metric.

## Match composition to meaning

| Information | Useful composition | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change over time | Aligned before/after or a shared-axis trend | Unrelated metrics in separate cards |
| Compare groups | Labelled bars, table, aligned small multiples | Different scales or colour-only legends |
| Process and ownership | Connected steps with clear AI/human lanes | Seven narrow text boxes with no relationship |
| Levels and progress | One ordered ladder with consistent scales | Unexplained height implying a second metric |
| Tradeoff or decision | Options beside criteria and consequence | Equal-weight feature grid hiding the choice |
| Instructions | Numbered actions, examples and completion feedback | Decorative hero before the task |
| A key claim | Statement with one piece of visible proof | Oversized slogan with no evidence |

Preserve numbers, denominators, time ranges, uncertainty and caveats together. Direct labels beat a distant legend. Start quantitative bars at zero unless a clearly labelled axis justifies otherwise. A zero is data; do not hide it.

## Rhythm and continuity

Alternate assertion, evidence and action when the story needs those beats. Dense evidence earns a quiet frame; a transition earns space. Repeating a layout can help comparisons: vary composition for a reason, not to satisfy a novelty quota.

Carry an existing object, position, label or colour into the next state when that helps orientation. A persistent object is optional; do not manufacture a phone or ticket to decorate an unrelated story. Motion should reveal a relationship, change or consequence. If removing it changes nothing about understanding, prefer stillness.

## Dense source, exact words

Preserve the claim/evidence pairing and section order. First improve width, line length, grouping, table alignment and available space. Use presenter-paced reveals only when they keep context available. Do not bury caveats in an undiscoverable final beat or move required reading into notes without authorization. If content cannot fit legibly, report the tradeoff and use an authorized additional view rather than silently cutting words.

## Inspect the result

Can a viewer explain the main claim after five seconds? Can they locate the supporting evidence? Does a visible next action follow? Is any contrast, layout or motion implying a claim the data does not make? Verify these on the actual rendering, not only in the source.
