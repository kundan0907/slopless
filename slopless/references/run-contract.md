# One run, one evidence trail

This contract supplies the execution order for every route. Quick changes the depth and scope, not the order. Review runs the same lenses without editing. The six-step design loop is the outer workflow: Interview supplies Audit inputs, Decide supplies Direction, Prove early/Build contain the nine passes, Verify contains Checklist, and Learn follows Report. The five visual-quality lenses judge the result; they are not another implementation order.

## Audit

Inspect the current artifact and record the target viewport, state, audience, task, content authority and existing components. New work starts from the supplied brief and its first real-content study; never invent a before image. Name the primary action or takeaway and its existing proof. Conversion work records the real funnel action and available baseline; no invented conversion lift or new tracking.

Each finding has `ID | view/state | rule | evidence | proposed change | acceptance check`. Evidence is a source location, rendered measurement or same-size before/after. Replace “premium”, “balanced” or “feels off” with a named defect: e.g. a clipped label, three equal-weight competing headings, a button's measured hit area, or generic copy that survives the competitor-name substitution.

Classify by confirmed findings, not a visual score:

- **None:** no confirmed defect in the inspected scope. Preserve the screen; passes record “preserve” with evidence. Suggestions stay in the report unless separately authorized. Do not manufacture a visible V2 for the Quick outcome paragraph.
- **Local:** every finding fits within at most three components/regions and no shared identity or page structure needs replacement. Fix those findings only; inherit direction.
- **Systemic:** findings require changes across more than three regions or a shared structural decision. An improvement request still does not authorize a redesign. Propose a direction and obtain authorization before widening scope.
- **Unverified:** rendering or evidence is missing. Source findings can be confirmed as source defects; visual severity and quality stay unknown.

Counts determine repair scope, not aesthetic worth. No finding authorizes changes to locked facts, features, prices, claims, URLs, section order or working navigation.

## Direction (approved)

Record Product, Character (two or three concrete words), World, Moments (zero to two), Signature (one product-derived detail), and the acceptance view. A material such as glass has a job: name the underlying layer it reveals, prove text contrast against its worst background, and provide an opaque fallback. Futuristic is an expression, not a feature or an excuse for illegible evidence.

**Taste calibration:** cite one actually inspected reference: a supplied screen, existing brand/component, bundled example or accessible real site. Record `reference | observed decision | target's current difference | principle borrowed | product-specific adaptation`. Inherited brand counts for a local repair. When World is generic or missing, use `reel-learnings.md#reel-3-taste`; open an accessible reference and name what it does differently. A URL alone is not inspection. If the reference cannot be opened, use an available inspected one or mark calibration blocked; do not claim observations from a name.

Record approval as `user choice`, `existing explicit brief/brand`, or `delegated: you pick/just do it`, citing its source. A vague “use slopless” does not approve a new visual identity. Ask only for a consequential unresolved decision. Review records `inherited, read-only`; it does not require approval to critique. No-slop preservation needs no new approval. A delegated one-shot brief allows the agent to choose and record direction before building without another confirmation round.

Run the competitor test on the idea: substitute another product's name. For authorized new work or redesign, if the idea still fits, name a distinctive fact from the supplied content and rebuild the proposed idea around it. For Review or local repairs, record a supported finding or an out-of-scope suggestion without rebuilding. A sound inherited direction can pass unchanged; no-slop preservation takes precedence over the competitor test. Borrow a reference's principle, not its branding, layout template or unsupported feature.

## Nine passes, in order

Finish each lens across the requested scope before advancing. Record `pass | changed / preserve / not applicable | evidence | acceptance result`. “Preserve” needs evidence; “not applicable” needs a specific absence (e.g. no icons). Do not create an icon, visual or animation to fill a row. Review records findings rather than making changes. Repairs revisit the failed pass and recheck downstream effects; they do not restart unrelated work.

| Pass | Checkable completion condition |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Spacing** | Repeated components use the same tokens and alignment; inner-group gaps are smaller than inter-group gaps. Inherit the project's grid; without one, use 4px steps with documented optical exceptions. Record a measured before/after for each spacing defect. |
| **2. Color** | Named surface/text/accent/semantic roles use existing or approved tokens. Normal text contrast is at least 4.5:1; large text and essential nontext controls at least 3:1. Test glass against its worst visible background. Semantic chart colors remain associated with their series. |
| **3. Icons** | Reuse the project's icon set and size/stroke tokens; compare repeated icon boxes and label baselines. Controls retain accessible names. Decorative icons need no replacement. |
| **4. Copy** | Check every changed string against authorized source. Label–value–unit, comparison period and caveat remain together. No new claims, prices, URLs, testimonials or features. Locked copy stays even if a scan dislikes it; record its exact exception. |
| **5. Type** | Record font roles, rendered sizes, weights and line breaks at the acceptance viewport. Inspect densest evidence and font-loaded state. A chosen brand font stays; a training-data font name alone is no defect. |
| **6. Layout** | One primary takeaway/action per view; name what leads and what supports it. Inspect reading order, overflow and 200% zoom/reflow where applicable. Check an affected narrow viewport; preserve approved deck aspect ratio and section order. |
| **7. Details** | Exercise affected focus, press, selected, disabled, loading and error states. Use the existing component kit; measure hit boxes, visible focus and nested radii. Web targets are at least 24×24 CSS px; touch targets at least 44×44. For small glyphs, enlarge the hit box beyond the visible glyph without overlap. |
| **8. Visuals** | Keep factual charts and supplied media exact. Every visual explains supplied content or an approved product action. Faux product UI, launch shots and announcement visuals follow the plan/poster/rehearsal method below. Otherwise record why that method is inapplicable. |
| **9. Motion** | Apply `motion.md#motion-contract` and all 20 rows there: cause, focus, poses, arcs, timing, frequency, interruption and reduced motion. Every authored UI tween is under 300ms; visible input acknowledgment is under 400ms. Verify computed values and playback, not source literals alone. |

**Supplied labels travel with their values (passes 4 and 6).** When the source already states a unit, period or status meaning for a value elsewhere on the same view (a legend, footnote or caption), placing that exact wording beside the value, in its column header or as a visible status label is a layout repair, not new copy. Do it when the distance hurts reading, keep the original statement unless removing it is authorized, and list the move under Content changes. Never infer a unit or meaning the source does not state for that exact value.

### Pass 8: plan the launch visual before code

Read `reel-learnings.md#reel-1-stageshow`. Write `stage/crop | focal object | action | payoff | omitted context | poster | beats`. Use three to five beats (cause → change → result), usually three to seven story-bearing strings. These budgets apply inside a fabricated product shot; never remove real screen, chart or deck evidence to meet them. Placeholder bars may simplify incidental faux UI only, never facts or functional labels.

Choose the closest available, actually inspected gold example. In a Stageshow project, film and copy its example folder, preserve its stage geometry/spacing/beat structure, then adapt product content, material and intent. Reuse its kit parts. In another stack, reuse the house components and translate the method; do not install React or Stageshow for a static HTML repair. Record the source and adaptations; a renamed example is insufficient.

Crop to the panel where the story happens. For a single window/phone shot, its projected bounding box occupies at least two thirds of the stage along its dominant dimension and intersects an edge; avoid an extra plate and four-sided empty margins. Inspect the still at the actual display size and 390px when that output is meant for mobile. This crop rule does not shrink real applications or crop away required deck content.

Set the settled poster/result first. It must communicate the takeaway with motion disabled. Rehearse playback frame by frame at each press, menu opening, morph and seam; inspect start, peak and end poses plus consecutive frames around every transition. Fix framing, then hierarchy, then motion, then polish; re-render the affected sequence. A contact sheet with only settled screenshots is not motion evidence.

When the Stageshow scripts are available, use their documented film command with `--gate`; do not claim its gate passed until its actual output says `rehearsal passed`. Otherwise record equivalent browser-frame inspection, timestamps, dimensions and faults; call it “manual frame rehearsal”, never a Stageshow gate. If recording/frames are unavailable, the launch visual is unverified. Keep optional video generation off unless requested.

## Checklist and scored gate

Run the existing source scanner on changed artifacts, plus the project's relevant tests/lint/typecheck. Confirm each candidate against context; record `rule | file:line | confirmed / intentional | reason`. An allowlist flag alone is not an exception record. The scanner's motion rules cover literal CSS durations and simple straight `offset-path` paths; JavaScript/framework source candidates are warnings until runtime confirmation; they do not parse all frameworks, runtime values, springs, JS timelines or composed transforms. Inspect those in playback and computed styles. Zero hits never proves design quality.

Checklist rows use `pass / fail / unverified / not applicable` with evidence or a reason:

- [ ] Audit findings have named rules and acceptance checks; no-slop regions remain unchanged.
- [ ] Direction approval source and inspected taste reference are recorded; competitor test names a product-specific fact.
- [ ] All nine passes appear in order, including evidence for preserved and inapplicable passes.
- [ ] Content, features, claims, prices, links, section order and behavior match authorized sources.
- [ ] Readability, contrast, responsive/zoom behavior, focus and affected controls pass at the stated viewing sizes.
- [ ] Launch/faux UI visual was planned before code; poster tells the story; frame-by-frame rehearsal inspected actions and seam.
- [ ] Authored spatial travel follows arcs, never straight lines; fades/scales have no travel path. Native direct manipulation stays under the user's control.
- [ ] Every authored UI animation is **<300ms**, including combined stagger completion; visible response is **<400ms**. No spring tail or chained tween hides a late response.
- [ ] Frequent actions use less motion than occasional actions; reduced motion reaches the same complete state.
- [ ] Scanner findings are confirmed or justified; relevant project checks ran; unavailable checks are explicitly unverified.
- [ ] Same-state before/after and motion evidence were actually inspected; source-only work has no visual score.

Borrow the split between deterministic gate and rendered judgment from unslop-review, not a claim that grep certifies design. After viewing the rendering, score five dimensions: **hierarchy, composition, reading, interaction/story, identity/fit**. Each is 0–4: **0** task/content blocked; **1** major defect; **2** confirmed local defects; **3** all applicable checks pass; **4** checks pass plus a measured before/after improvement against the stated acceptance check. Cite evidence for each score. No render means `score: unverified`, never zero disguised as an inspected result. No-change screens can ship at 3; they need no forced upgrade to earn 4. This adapted 20-point rubric is not the upstream 100-point score.

Disposition is derived in this order:

1. **recapture**: any required evidence or project check is missing, unavailable or unverified, including rendering and playback; list exactly what must be captured or rerun. A score cannot substitute.
2. **fix**: evidence exists and any required checklist item fails, any confirmed material finding remains, or any dimension is below 3. Suggestions beyond authorized scope remain suggestions.
3. **ship**: every applicable checklist item passes, each dimension is at least 3, all required project checks passed, and no material finding remains. “Ship” covers only the inspected scope; it does not authorize publication.

Never use an average to hide content loss, unreadable evidence, broken navigation or missing accessibility. Runtime or device performance is unverified until measured on the relevant environment. An unavailable project check prevents a full ship claim; report the narrower result.

## Report

Use `report.md`, followed by the compact evidence trail: audit severity and findings; approved Direction and taste observation; nine pass results; checklist failures/unverified rows; five evidence-linked scores or `unverified`; disposition; exact checks and inspected viewports/states; remaining limits. Link a longer record when necessary rather than writing a scoring essay. Describe conversion improvements as hypotheses until real funnel data exists. No skill can guarantee universal taste or conversion.
